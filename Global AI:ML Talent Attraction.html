Flagship — Function Redesign

Rebuilding Global AI/ML Talent Attraction

The organization was active, but the activity wasn't aligned with business demand: heavy reliance on inbound and agency pipelines, conference investment measured by attendance and resume volume instead of qualified yield, limited visibility into where representation dropped across the funnel, and time-to-fill that swung widely across ML engineering, research science, MLOps, SRE, and AI infrastructure. What the function needed wasn't more recruiting activity — it needed an architecture connecting talent demand, attraction channels, recruiter execution, data, inclusion, and business outcomes. Rebuilt around four areas:

01

Conference Ecosystem Design

Segmented conferences by talent community — research, applied ML, and smaller technical circles like MLOps, SRE, and distributed systems — and pre-identified researchers, accepted-paper authors, and community leaders before each event, so conversations were scheduled, not left to booth traffic. Partnered with ERGs to embed inclusion directly into the engagement strategy, not as a bolt-on. Added intake calibration, structured lead tagging, clear recruiter ownership, and a 48-hour follow-up standard.

22% → 59% event-to-applicant conversion
02

Funnel Diagnostics & Source-Mix Health

Built dashboards tracking qualified conversion by source, representation by stage, candidate velocity, and yield by domain. Found a marquee AI conference converting resumes at ~12% qualified, while a smaller specialized ML community converted near 40% — evidence that changed how leadership thought about "big" versus "right," and redirected budget by skill domain.

12% vs. ~40% qualified conversion, by source
03

Responsible AI-Enabled Sourcing

Piloted market-mapping and screening tools to sharpen signal in competitive AI talent markets — deliberately without scaling automation ahead of governance. Reviewed pass-through rates across candidate groups, checked keyword logic and institutional preferences for unintended bias, required human validation before priority-pipeline placement, and benchmarked tool output against interview scorecards and hiring outcomes.

Augmentation with human accountability, not automation for its own sake
04

Operating Discipline & Accountability

Leading a global team of 10, established program ownership, common success measures, portfolio reviews, and escalation paths — and coached the team to report the business problem, hypothesis, data, and outcome, instead of activity counts alone.

Recruiting CoP: SharePoint + Copilot Hub

The recruiting community had no single, current source of truth spanning the hiring lifecycle. Built a SharePoint intranet hub structured around the eight phases of the recruiting lifecycle, then layered Copilot on top so updates and announcements reach the community fast — instead of sitting in email threads or scattered decks.

Rolled out as a hub-and-spoke model, with regional spokes for NAMR, EMEA, and APAC feeding from — and back into — the central hub, so regional teams get locally relevant content without fragmenting the source of truth.

Why it matters

One governed hub instead of three disconnected regional processes; Copilot cuts the lag between "this changed" and "the field knows."

Fig. 02 — Talent Retention

Internal Mobility → AI/ML Pipeline

As the org shifted toward AI/ML, engineering talent risked being left behind — or leaving. Built a monthly program where a different line-of-business AI/ML leader presents live: what their team is building, what roles are open, and what a strong candidate for those roles actually looks like.

Internal employees — largely software engineers exploring an AI/ML move — sign up, attend, and ask questions directly tied to open requisitions: what to work on, what to say in the interview, how to frame their experience.

Impact

Before this program, software engineers had low success moving into AI/ML roles and struggled to position their experience. Since launch, that transition success rate has risen.

Fig. 03 — Recruiter Development

Recruiter Onboarding & Performance Program

Recruiter onboarding had no structured, AI-enabled path — new recruiters learned the job piecemeal. Built a formal onboarding program that incorporates AI tooling from day one, replacing an area that hadn't existed before.

Separately, recruiter performance had gone flat: no clear way for recruiters to see how to grow or what "better" looked like. Built a companion program giving recruiters a path to develop and a reason to invest in their own performance.

Impact

In last year's performance reviews, recruiters documented substantially more detail on the side projects they'd taken on to improve their own recruiting — a marked shift from the flat, undifferentiated reviews before the program.

Fig. 04 — Pipeline Generation

Referral Program & Career Fair

Referring wasn't easy — the friction was in "how does the person I know actually get into the system." Redesigned the path around QR codes and minimal-information capture, so a conversation at an event turns into a real candidate record in seconds, not a multi-step internal form.

Hosted a referral career fair with live job postings: attendees scan a QR code, give minimal info, and a recruiter follows up on the right open role. Separately, built relationships with recently hired PhD leaders whose university networks — direct lines to professors — extended the pipeline further, including a recent Stanford-tied internship event.

Why it matters

Removes the biggest drop-off point in referrals: effort. Fewer steps between "I know someone great" and a role in front of them.

Fig. 05 — Compliant Sourcing

LinkedIn Sourcing, Built Around Compliance

Working at a highly regulated company means AI cannot be integrated directly with LinkedIn — so the fix had to be procedural, not automated. Built a campaign and a single non-AI tool integration that makes it fast and simple for hiring teams and employees to create a post and ship it, without staring at a blank box wondering what to say.

Recruiters receive a quarterly report on their own LinkedIn activity and the projects tied to it. Click-through from LinkedIn to the careers site, and hires attributed back to that traffic, are tracked — because even with heavy passive sourcing, candidates end up checking a LinkedIn profile regardless.

Why it matters

Solved for compliance first, then for ease-of-use — proof that sourcing programs don't need AI automation to be effective, just a well-designed process.

Fig. 06 — Interview Design

Interview Process & "Super Days"

Built the interview process from the ground up for SRE roles, then extended the same design to AI/ML roles as hiring shifted. The centerpiece was "Super Days" — condensed, high-touch interview events that replaced a slower, more fragmented process, and one of the execution pieces behind the conference ecosystem redesign in the flagship case study above.

Impact

Super Days is part of what drove the 22% → 59% event-to-applicant conversion gain detailed above.

Fig. 06 — Interview-to-outcome conversion, Super Days rollout
Fig. 07 — Enablement Layer

Recruiting LLM Copilot Training, by Phase

Recruiters were spending real time just figuring out what to ask the model — reinventing prompts from scratch for every phase of a req. Built a full LLM Copilot training program structured around each phase of the recruiting lifecycle, so the prompt for "sourcing outreach" or "offer negotiation" already exists and just needs the details dropped in.

Integrated an HTML output layer on top of the prompt library, so what a recruiter generates and sends — to a hiring manager or directly to a candidate — renders as a polished, formatted document instead of raw model text.

Why it matters

Removes prompt-writing as a skill bottleneck and raises the visual bar on every hiring-manager and candidate touchpoint, without recruiters needing any HTML or prompting expertise themselves.
